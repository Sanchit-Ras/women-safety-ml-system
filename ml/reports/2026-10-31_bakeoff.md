# Forecasting bake-off (2026-10-31)

611 stable units, 8,820 windows with label years 2008-2022, 27 features from the 6 changes before each label year. Target: next year's change of the unit's total, scored in percent (median 3.9%, p1 -60.2%, p99 149.9%). 557 further windows were left out because a year they touch had more than 50% of the unit's total estimated across its boundary (a unit not yet separate is a fixed share of its parent: its changes are the parent's, and a forecast would be graded against an estimate). Among the windows kept, the unit carried an estimate across its boundary in 453 (over 5% of the total) and 126 (over 20%).

Rolling origin: each target year is forecast by a model trained only on earlier label years. Settings were chosen on 2016, 2017, 2018 alone; the headline is 2019, 2020, 2021, 2022; 2020 and 2021 are nationwide shock years (lockdown and rebound), shown and also left out.

## Reading

**Size of next year's change.** By the rule (lowest validation MAE) the point-forecast contender is **stacked**. On the test years it scores MAE 27.7 against 26.6 for the best baseline (zero): skill -0.042, R2 0.068, MAE difference +0.6 to +1.6 points, not a win beyond the noise.
**Which units will be in the top tenth of growth.** By the rule (highest validation precision at 10%) the ranking contender is **hotspot**: on the test years 0.367 of the units it flags were in the top tenth, interval 0.312 to 0.421, against 0.100 by chance: a real signal. But ranking by size alone, smallest first, scores 0.271: percentage growth favours tiny units (1 case to 3 is +200%), so much of that ranking is size. The next section removes size.
